Open items before submission
============================

prior work and what is new
--------------------------
  Pedley (1968) gives the impulse law for a toroidal bubble, the
  hollow-core speed, the viscous diffusion of vorticity from the core,
  the capillary stability criterion (from Pedley 1967) and a breakup
  time; he also integrated the Boyle-law impulse equation numerically
  for one laboratory bubble and found a 7 percent effect, which the
  present model reproduces (verification.txt). Ruban and Rasmussen
  (2003) treat an adiabatic gas core variationally and numerically;
  their Eq. 53 is the core balance used here. Cheng, Lou and Lim
  (2013) simulate viscous bubble rings at Re = 30 to 100 with constant
  gas volume and show breakup through azimuthal surface-tension
  instability of short waves (k a near 2), the band the filament model
  projects out. Chang and Llewellyn Smith (2018) solve a tilted
  buoyant filament and obtain R sin(theta) = const; the present
  closure for a massless gas core gives R^2 sin(theta) = const.
  Claimed as new: the exact Boyle solution and universal gain, their
  growth with release depth, the tilt law and lateral drift, the
  swirl-head bound, the R^-1/2 decay of long bending waves, and
  Pedley's breakup criterion evaluated along Boyle trajectories.

physics left out
----------------
  Inviscid liquid: circulation is held fixed, although viscous
  diffusion from the free-slip core surface and turbulence decay it in
  practice; the breakup depths of figure 4b use Pedley's estimate of
  that decay at the core surface only. No gas dissolution. Uniform
  core cross-section along the filament, justified while the vertical
  extent of the core stays below the swirl head (figure 4d). No
  core-scale dynamics: bending waves with k a > 1, where the
  Widnall-type short-wave instability lives, are projected out, so the
  breakup itself is not computed. Free-surface interaction above 0.5 m
  is not modeled.

parameters
----------
  Gas volume, radius and circulation of diver-blown rings are
  illustrative. The results stated as ratios (gain over constant
  volume, flaring, the tilt law, the R^-1/2 wobble decay) do not
  depend on them; absolute radii, times and drifts do. A pool test
  with a camera at two depths would fix R0, V0 and Gamma and test the
  tilt law directly.
